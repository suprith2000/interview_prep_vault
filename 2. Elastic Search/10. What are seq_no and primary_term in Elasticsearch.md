
In Elasticsearch, `seq_no` (sequence number) and `primary_term` are essential components of the document versioning and conflict resolution mechanism. They play a crucial role in maintaining data consistency, especially in distributed environments. Here’s a detailed explanation of both:

### 1. **seq_no (Sequence Number)**

- **Definition**: The `seq_no` is a unique identifier for each operation (index, update, delete) performed on a document within a shard. It is incremented with each write operation on that specific document.
    
- **Purpose**:
    
    - **Version Control**: Each time a document is created, updated, or deleted, its `seq_no` is incremented. This allows Elasticsearch to track the order of operations for a document.
    - **Conflict Detection**: During concurrent updates, the `seq_no` helps detect conflicts. When an update is attempted, Elasticsearch checks whether the provided `seq_no` matches the current one for that document. If they don't match, it means another operation has occurred since the original read, and the operation is rejected to prevent overwriting changes.
- **Behavior**:
    
    - The `seq_no` is unique within the primary shard. Each primary shard maintains its own sequence numbers independently of other shards.
    - When a document is replicated to a replica shard, the `seq_no` is also copied to ensure consistency between the primary and replica shards.

### 2. **primary_term**

- **Definition**: The `primary_term` is a versioning identifier that indicates the primary term of the primary shard that holds the document. It is incremented each time the primary shard is elected or a new primary shard is created due to a node failure or recovery.
    
- **Purpose**:
    
    - **Shard Leadership**: The `primary_term` helps keep track of the primary shard's current term and is critical for maintaining consistency during shard relocations or failovers.
    - **Consistency Across Replica Shards**: The `primary_term` is used in conjunction with `seq_no` to ensure that updates are only applied from the current primary shard. It helps prevent updates from stale replicas that may not have the most recent data.
- **Behavior**:
    
    - The `primary_term` is incremented each time the primary shard is elected (for example, if the primary shard goes down and a new primary is elected).
    - During an update operation, Elasticsearch checks both the `seq_no` and the `primary_term`. An update is allowed only if the `seq_no` matches the latest value for that document and the `primary_term` matches the current primary term for the shard.

### Example Scenario

To illustrate how `seq_no` and `primary_term` work together, consider the following sequence of operations on a document:

1. A document is indexed into a primary shard, which assigns it a `seq_no` of 1 and a `primary_term` of 1.
2. An update is made to the document, which increments the `seq_no` to 2, while the `primary_term` remains at 1.
3. If a failover occurs, and the primary shard becomes unavailable, a new primary shard is elected, incrementing the `primary_term` to 2. The `seq_no` would start again from 1 for this new primary shard.
4. If an update is attempted on the document using the old `seq_no` and `primary_term` (which are now stale), Elasticsearch will reject the operation due to a mismatch, ensuring data consistency.

### Conclusion

- **`seq_no`**: Tracks the order of write operations for documents within a shard, enabling conflict detection during concurrent updates.
- **`primary_term`**: Indicates the current primary shard's term, helping maintain consistency during shard relocations and ensuring that updates are applied from the correct primary shard.

Together, `seq_no` and `primary_term` provide a robust mechanism for version control and conflict resolution in Elasticsearch, crucial for operating in a distributed environment.
